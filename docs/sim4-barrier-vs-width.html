Does just making the network wider make the merging problem go away?

Prior work (Entezari et al.; Ainsworth et al.) reports that permutation matching works well on wide networks but leaves a barrier on thin ones. This page shows what happens to the barrier between two independently trained networks as width grows, on one task (coffee roasting), with and without the sign-flip fix.

PAPERMedians over independent pairs on the coffee-roasting task (Study 1, Appendix A Table 8). Barrier = training-error points above the straight line between the two networks. Axis starts at zero.

Expert notes

Modes
none: no alignment. wm: weight matching over permutations (Hungarian). wm_sign: matching with sign flips, objective Σ|uiA⊤uπ(i)B|. bf_perm / bf_sign: brute force over permutations / permutations and flips, chosen by midpoint loss.
Reading the result
With flips, the barrier is removed at widths 3 and 4 (upper quartiles 0.04 and 0.11) but not from width 6 to 64 (upper quartiles 0.77–1.30). Flips still lower the barrier at every width.
Caveat from the paper
At widths 4, 6 and 8 nearly every network has two units on almost the same line, so near-symmetries outside the finite group may remain; the exhaustive search does not cover them.
Scope
One task, one hidden layer, sigmoid. Study 1 rules were recorded in a private notebook (Section 3).
